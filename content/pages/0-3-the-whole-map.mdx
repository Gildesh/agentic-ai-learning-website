## The idea

Part 1 explains generative models: they predict text, they can be confidently wrong, and they only see what you put in front of them. Part 2 is how you ask: role, task, context, constraints, and the shape of the answer. Part 3 is the tools that wrap a model so it can edit a project: VS Code, Cursor, Google Antigravity, and Claude Code. Part 4 is the fast loop people call vibe coding, and the wall where a pretty screen is still fake. Part 5 is the written instructions an <Term id="agent">agent</Term> needs: rules files, skills, and a spec.

Part 6 is the <Term id="full-stack">full-stack</Term> map: browser, server, and where the app is hosted. Part 7 is how programs talk (HTTP, JSON) and how rows live in a database. You have already seen one page of that part, on methods and status codes. Part 8 is secrets, users, and payments. Part 9 is the UI libraries you will meet in real repos: React, Next.js, and a short look at Angular, Vue, and Svelte.

Part 10 is how one prompt cannot build a product, and how a loop of spec, task, build, check, and commit can. Part 11 is when to use a strong model and when to use a fast one. Part 12 is how a rule becomes a check the agent cannot talk its way past: linters, tests, and hooks. The appendix is the glossary, cheat sheets, a launch list, and a capstone that uses the loop on a small paid app.
