<Hook>

The demo goes well. You click Pay and the page says Paid. A friend nods. The next morning you look at the only function behind the button. It returns a paragraph. It never calls a payment service. The prototype looked finished because the sentence on the screen was the sentence you asked to see.

</Hook>

## The idea

Here is the session, compressed, with the checks that were skipped.

You were <Term id="vibe-coding">vibe coding</Term> a checkout for Paper Lantern. The asks were small at first: three titles, then a tax line of 80 cents, then a Pay button. The third reaction was "when I click Pay, show Paid." The agent did exactly that. The <Term id="hallucination">hallucination</Term> was not a weird library name. It was a true-looking sentence with no source. You had requested the sentence. You had not requested a charge.

The function was the four-line handler from the previous page. A reading pass would have stopped the demo from being described as a checkout. A network tab in the browser would have shown one request, to your own page, and none to a payment host. Either check takes a minute. Neither was in the loop, because the loop ended when the screen matched the ask.

The data risk sits beside the fake success. If the form includes a card number field, the browser will send that number to your server, because that is what a form does. A prototype must not render that field. "Show Paid" is a safe ask. "Take the card and show Paid" is how a demo starts collecting real card numbers into a log you have not read.

<Term id="git">Git</Term> is how you keep the titles and drop the button. If you committed after the tax line and left the Pay experiment uncommitted, `git restore` on the handler file returns you to the list and the tax, without the lying button. If you committed the button because the demo felt done, you now have a snapshot of a false checkout. Revert that on purpose before anyone copies the folder.

Nothing here requires a villain. The agent obeyed. The screen was the spec you wrote. The spec was incomplete.

## Real example

The ask that created the gap:

```
When I click Pay, show the word Paid.
```

The ask that would have failed honestly:

```
When I click Pay, show Paid only if a payment request returns a success status.
If you cannot call a payment service, show the words "not connected" and do not add a card field.
```

The second ask cannot be satisfied by returning a paragraph. That is why it is the better spec for anything you might keep.

<TryIt minutes={5}>

Write the Pay handler as those four lines in a file, open it, and say what a click does. Then write the honest ask above. You do not need a payment account. You need to see that "show Paid" and "a payment succeeded" are different sentences.

</TryIt>

<Mistake>

Narrating the demo as "checkout works" because the room saw the word Paid. Say what you tested: "the page can render a sentence." If you tested a charge, you can point at the payment response. If you cannot, you did not test a charge.

</Mistake>
