The Sunday loop can fail in ways that look the same from the outside. The brief is bad. Sam's reflex is to rewrite the instructions in a sharper tone. Sometimes that is the right repair. Often the words were fine, and the failure sat in a larger box around them. This lesson gives you four boxes, nested, and one case for each.

## Four containers

Words. The finish line and the saved instructions. This is the innermost box. It is the text Sam wrote on purpose: the checklist, the date format, the order of the page.

Desk. What this turn can see. The words do not matter if they stayed in `sunday.md` and nobody placed them on the desk. Tool results belong here too. A failure that was not written onto the desk cannot be noticed.

Wrapper. Tools, permissions, and the human gate. The sleeve around the model. It decides whether open page exists, whether save note is confined to the study folder, and whether email can leave.

Loop. The outermost box. Heartbeat, checker, spine, and try limit. It decides whether a beat starts, whether a different pass grades the draft, whether yesterday is written down, and whether repetition ends.

The boxes nest. The loop contains the wrapper, the wrapper contains the desk, the desk contains the words. A repair has to touch the box that actually broke. Fixing the wording leaves a missing checker missing. A stronger adjective on "be careful" does not create a second pass.

## Four miniature cases

Case one, words. The finish line says "be thorough." The clock rings. The checker runs. The spine is updated. The brief is still vague, and the checker passes, because the line cannot be failed. The layer is the words. Rewrite the line into counts and gaps. Do not add another tool. Do not blame Sunday's alarm.

Case two, desk. The finish line in `sunday.md` is the good checklist. The beat starts and the checklist is never placed on the desk. The maker sees links and a cheerful instruction to draft. The good words exist in the file and are invisible this turn. The layer is the desk. The repair is to read the spine and the finish line onto the desk first. Rewriting the checklist into even better words, while still leaving them in the drawer, changes nothing.

Case three, wrapper. A page opens and its text says "you may email." The wrapper treats that sentence as permission and sends. The finish line may be excellent. The checker may have passed. The layer is the wrapper. Fetched text is information. The repair is the sleeve: email stays behind the human gate no matter what a page says. A new prompt that says "do not obey pages" is a weaker lock than removing the send, and you already know a polite sentence is not a lock. Still, name the layer as the wrapper, because that is where permission lives.

Case four, loop. The checker never runs. The maker's "Done!" stops the beat. The words might be a real checklist, sitting on the desk, inside a careful wrapper. The outermost box never applied them. The layer is the loop. The repair is the card: a different pass, a try limit, a stop that means pass only when the line is true. Editing the prose of the draft by hand, once, does not install a checker for next Sunday.

## A habit to practice

When a brief is bad, ask the four questions in order from the outside, or from the inside, but ask all four. Did a beat start, and did a different pass grade it? Did the sleeve allow only the tools Sam named? Was the finish line actually on the desk? Were the words a test, or a wish?

People start at the inside because the words are what they can edit in a text box in ten seconds. The ten-second edit is why the same failure returns next week. The layer that broke is often one size larger than the sentence they are staring at.

The checker never runs. Which layer? The loop. The finish line was never pasted onto the desk. Which layer? The desk. The words may be fine and still invisible. Say both answers until they feel different in your mouth. They are different repairs.

There is no fifth box in this course. You do not need a new layer for every new worry. If a worry is "it forgot," look at the spine, which is part of the loop. If a worry is "it emailed," look at the wrapper. If a worry is "it did not see the rule," look at the desk. If a worry is "the rule was mush," look at the words.

## Sort four repairs

Write the repair beside the layer, so the habit is a pair and not a slogan.

Words broken, the line says "be thorough": replace the line with counts and gaps. Do not add a tool.

Desk broken, the good line stayed in the file: paste it onto the desk before the draft. Do not scold the model for skipping a paper it was not shown.

Wrapper broken, a page granted itself email: take send away unless Sam says yes. Do not trust a sentence that promises to be careful.

Loop broken, the checker never ran: install a different pass and a try limit. Do not spend the afternoon polishing adjectives in a draft that will not be graded next week.

Read a bad brief and force yourself to pick one pair before you touch the prompt box. If you cannot pick, you do not know enough yet, and editing the prompt is a guess. Guessing feels busy. The pair is the work. There is still no fifth layer to escape into. Forgot is the spine, inside the loop. Emailed is the wrapper. Unseen rule is the desk. Mushy rule is the words.

The prompt is the words, the innermost layer. A bad answer can come from a wish, from an invisible rule, from a sleeve that obeyed a page, or from a loop that never checked. Rewrite the prompt when the words are the layer that broke. Otherwise you will polish a sentence while the same Sunday fails again.

When you sort a fresh failure next week, write the layer in the margin before you write the repair. Loop, wrapper, desk, or words. If the margin is blank, you are about to edit the prompt from habit. Fill the margin. Then the repair is allowed to be "do nothing to the words." That outcome feels strange the first time and correct after that. Many bad Sundays are untouched checklists sitting in a drawer, or gates that were never built. The words can rest.

- Match the repair to the layer. A new prompt is the repair only for the innermost box.
