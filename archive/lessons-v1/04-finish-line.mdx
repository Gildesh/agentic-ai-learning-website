Sam says, "Make a good summary." The helper replies, "Done!" and offers a paragraph. Sam reads it. It is smooth. It might be good. Sam cannot tell whether to accept it. "Good" is a mood. "Done" is the helper's mood about its own paragraph. Neither one is a mark in the margin.

## What you can mark

A finish line is a pass/fail test a second person could apply without asking the model. The second person does not need to be clever. They need a rule they can point at. If two careful people can disagree forever, the line is still a wish.

Walk three pairs. Say the bad line out loud, then the good line, and notice which one you could grade with a pen.

Bad: "high quality." Good: "one page; each source named; one sentence per source; every claim has a source." A second person can count the pages, count the sentences, and underline any claim that names no source. They do not ask the model whether it feels proud.

Bad: "be helpful." Good: "answer the three questions Sam wrote, in that order." Helpful depends on Sam's mood that hour. The three questions are on paper. Question two either has an answer under it or it does not.

Bad: "research strawberries well." Good: "name three differences between a garden strawberry and a wild strawberry, each with a source a person can open." "Well" cannot fail. Three differences can fail if there are two, or if a difference has no source, or if the source is a sentence that does not open.

Keep the good lines boring. Boring is a feature. A finish line that sounds like a slogan will be graded like a slogan, which is to say it will not be graded.

## Sam's brief, rewritten

Put Sam's wish and a real finish line next to each other.

Wish: make a good summary of my three links.

Finish line: one page; each source named; one sentence per source; every claim has a source; if a link did not open, the page contains a written gap that names the link.

Read the second one as a checklist. Page count. Names. One sentence each. No orphan claims. A gap line where a link failed. You could hand that checklist to a friend who has not seen the chat. Your friend could mark pass or fail. That is the test of the test.

Notice what the finish line does not say. It does not say the summary must be wise. Wisdom can be a later conversation between people. The finish line is the part a loop, or a friend, can apply while you are asleep or while you are still learning to read the articles yourself. If you hide wisdom inside the line, you have smuggled the wish back in.

## A second wish, from a different chore

Sam's friend says, "Make the notes better." You will meet this sentence again as a check. Practice the move now. "Better" asks the reader to share a taste. Replace it with counts and shapes. One possible line: five bullets, each under twenty words, each ending with a source name. A friend can count the bullets, count the words, and look at the last words of each bullet. They never ask the model if the notes feel better.

Another chore: "Write a kind email." Kind is a wish. A finish line might be: four sentences; the first names the homework; the second asks one question; no sentence tells the teacher they are wrong. You can still argue about tone. You can no longer argue about whether a question was asked. Put the arguable part in the person's review. Put the countable part in the finish line.

When you rewrite, keep the person's real aim. Sam does want a summary that is worth reading. The finish line is not a replacement for taste. It is the part taste can stand on. A page that names its sources can still be dull. A page that cannot show its sources cannot be trusted, however bright the prose is. Trust is the pass/fail. Brightness is a later edit.

## How this sits beside the three kinds of help

A recipe often is a finish line that has already been turned into steps: always save the date as 12 May. An agent needs a finish line because it chooses, and a chooser can stop in a place the person did not want. A chat can live without one, because you are there every turn to say "not that." The moment you want to look away, you need the line written down. You are not building that look-away system yet. You are learning to write the line so that, when you do, the system has something other than "Done!" to obey.

Read your own line once more and ask: could a person mark this without asking the model? If the honest answer is no, the line is still a wish wearing a neat shirt.

## Grade a stranger's wish

Take three wishes you have heard this month. They might be "clean this up," "make it pop," or "look into it." Rewrite each as a line a stranger could mark on paper without calling you. For "clean this up," a possible line is: remove repeated sentences, keep the three names, and leave every number unchanged. A stranger can see a repeated sentence, can count names, and can compare numbers to the original. They cannot see "clean."

Read your rewrites and strike any word you could not fail. Quality, nice, deep, and some are strike words. Numbers, names, order, and "or a written line that says what is missing" can stay. If a rewrite still needs your taste, move the taste into a note that says "Sam will read this after the line passes." The line stays markable. The taste stays human. That split will matter when a checker exists. The checker gets the line. You keep the taste.

<Misconception>

A goal is a wish written politely.

</Misconception>

Please, kindly, and thoroughly are manners. A finish line is a test. Manners do not become a test because they sound careful. "Be thorough" cannot fail in a way two people would agree on. "Three sources, or a written line that names the missing one" can.

<Check question="Rewrite make the notes better into a finish line someone could mark pass or fail.">

Five bullets, each under twenty words, each ending with a source name. A person can count the bullets, the words, and the source names without asking the model.

</Check>

<Check question="Which line can a checker mark without asking the model: be thorough, or three sources or a written line that says which source is missing?">

The second. "Be thorough" cannot fail a shared test. A count of sources, or a written gap, can.

</Check>

Read your three rewrites once more the next morning, when you are less attached to them. Strike one leftover mood word if it survived the night. A finish line that still makes sense when you are sleepy is the one a checker can use. A line that only makes sense while you remember what you meant is still a private wish.

<Recap>

- A finish line is a pass/fail test a second person can apply.
- "Good," "helpful," and "well" are wishes. Counts, names, and order are tests.
- Walk the bad line and the good line out loud before you trust a goal.
- Taste can come later. The line is the part you can mark while you are away.

</Recap>
