The brief gets worse on a Tuesday. Sam wants to be helpful to the helper, so Sam pastes five old chats "for background." They are about a different class, a trip, a complaint about a grade, and two half-finished briefs from other weeks. The finish line is in there somewhere, near the bottom of the paste. The helper writes a page that mentions the trip, misses the gap for link 3, and sounds thorough. Sam had added more. The draft got worse.

## What the desk is for

The desk holds three kinds of paper, and you already met them as a picture. Instructions, including the finish line. The recent conversation. Tool results, such as the text of link 1, the text of link 2, and the failure of link 3.

That is the pile a beat is allowed to see. Text that is not on the desk does not exist for this turn. A brilliant note in a closed drawer, a sentence fifty messages above the slice the product sends, a fact Sam "basically said last week" — none of those are in the continuation unless they were placed where this turn can read them.

Extra text is not neutral. The model continues a pattern from the whole pile. A pile that is mostly old chats will continue the pattern of those chats. The finish line becomes one page among many, easy to skip, the way a single instruction is easy to skip in a noisy room. The missing source is harder to notice when the first paper on the desk is a travel plan and the finish line is buried under it.

## Two desks, same finish line

Desk one. The first paper is the finish line, in Sam's short checklist. Under it: the failure of link 3, the text of link 1, the text of link 2, and Sam's sentence "draft the page." A person glancing at the top sees the test. The helper's next continuation has the test in the brightest spot.

Desk two. The first papers are five old chats. The finish line is pasted after them, unlabeled. The tool results are pasted after that. A glance hits the trip. The continuation has more material that is about the trip than about the gap line. "More background" felt like kindness. It buried the test.

Which desk makes a missing source easier to notice? The first. The finish line has to be visible. Visible means near the work, not merely somewhere in a long scroll that a person could find if they hunted. If you are unsure the product shows the whole scroll to the model, put the finish line again, immediately above the question.

## What does not belong, even if it is true

Sam's old complaint about a grade may be true. It is not part of this brief. Truth is not a ticket onto the desk. Relevance is. A tool result from yesterday is true in the sense that it happened, and it is still unusable if it is not pasted into this turn. Can this turn use it? No. Yesterday's result has to be copied onto today's desk, or today's tool has to fetch it again and put a fresh result down.

This is the same rule as the closed drawer, applied to clutter. The drawer was absence. The five chats are presence of the wrong papers. Both leave the finish line unread. Absence and clutter are cousins. One withholds the test. The other hides it.

When you prepare a desk, do a cruel edit. Keep the finish line. Keep the tool results this beat needs. Keep the newest instruction. Take out the trip, the grade, and the other weeks. If a detail from an old chat matters, copy that detail as one line under the finish line. Do not paste the chat in order to be fair to your past self. The model is not your past self. It is a continuation of whatever pile you built.

You are not learning a system that searches a library for you. You are learning to set a table. A beat reads the table. Set it so the test is the first thing a tired reader would see.

## Set the table for one draft

Before you ask for a draft, write the desk as a list you could paste, in this order. Line 1 is the finish line, the full checklist, not the word summary. Line 2 is the failure of link 3, in one sentence. Line 3 is a short extract from link 1, or a note that you do not have the extract yet. Line 4 is the same for link 2. Line 5 is the instruction: draft the page. Stop. Do not add the trip. Do not add last month's grade. Do not add a chat that was "for context."

Read the list as if you were a tired stranger. Can you see the test without scrolling? Can you see that link 3 failed without inferring it from silence? If the answer is no, the desk is not ready, and a worse draft will not be the model's mysterious mood. It will be the table you set. After the draft arrives, check it only against line 1. Extra charm that is not required by line 1 is optional. A missing gap is a fail, even if the prose about link 1 is lovely. You are practicing a small cruelty that protects the test.

<Misconception>

More background always helps.

</Misconception>

Background helps when it is the paper the finish line needs. Extra chats crowd the desk and make the test easier to skip. A larger pile is not a clearer pile.

If your pasted list is longer than a screen, it is probably clutter again. Cut it until the finish line, the failure, and the extracts fit without scrolling on a phone. A phone-sized desk is a good test of visibility. If the test is off the bottom of the screen, it is easy to skip, for a person and for a continuation. Shorten the extracts. Keep the checklist whole. The whole checklist is the point of line 1. An extract can be three sentences. The test cannot be a hint.

After you cut the list, paste it into a blank note and read only line 1 and line 2. Those two lines should tell you the test and the failure without any article text. If they do, the desk is carrying the lesson. The extracts support the sentences. They do not replace the test. When a draft later wanders, you will know whether the wander came from a buried line or from a line that was never a test. Today the practice is the short list itself.

<Recap>

- The desk holds instructions, the recent conversation, and tool results.
- The finish line belongs at the front, where a missing source is easy to see.
- Old chats can be true and still be clutter.
- A result that is not pasted is a closed drawer, even if you remember it.

</Recap>
