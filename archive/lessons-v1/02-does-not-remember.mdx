On Sunday night Sam and a chat plan a study week. They name three articles. They agree the brief should be one page. Sam feels understood. Sam closes the laptop.

Monday morning Sam opens a new chat and types "continue from yesterday." The reply is polite and empty. It asks what Sam would like to continue. The plan is gone. The articles are gone. The feeling of being understood was a feeling about a conversation that is no longer on the table.

## What the morning chat can see

The model continues text, which you already know. The new fact is about the pile of text it continues from. Each turn, it sees only what is placed in front of it. That pile is the desk. On the desk in a fresh chat there is Sam's new sentence, and whatever short instruction the product puts there by itself. There is no yesterday, because yesterday was a different pile, and that pile was cleared when the tab closed.

Say it in the smallest words. The model sees the desk, not the drawer.

The drawer is the file Sam could have written: three links, the one-page rule, the questions the brief must answer. The file can sit on the laptop all night. The model does not reach into the laptop because a file exists. Someone has to open the drawer and put the note on the desk. In this lesson, "someone" is Sam, pasting the note into the new chat. Later, a tool might place a note on the desk. The rule will be the same. If it is not on the desk, this turn cannot use it.

Look at the picture before you read on. Three papers sit on the desk: instructions, this conversation, and a pasted note. A broom is labeled "new chat." A closed drawer is labeled as a file the model cannot see until someone opens it. The broom is not a punishment. It is what a new chat is. The drawer is not the model's memory. It is a place a person, or later a tool, can copy from.

## A second morning

Imagine Sam does paste the note. The note says: three links, one page, every claim needs a source. Now the desk holds that note plus the new question, "draft the brief." The model can continue from those papers. It still does not remember Sunday. It is reading what Sam put in front of it on Monday. If the paste left out one link, that link is as gone as if it had never been discussed. The model will not say "you forgot the third link" unless the third link is somewhere on the desk to be noticed.

This is why "it knows me" is the wrong comfort. A product may store chats on a server so you can reopen an old thread. Reopening the old thread puts that thread back on the desk. That is the product fetching a record and placing it in view. It is not a mind that kept you overnight. If you start a new thread and paste nothing, you are a stranger with one sentence, even if you have used the same login for a year.

People meet a softer version of the same surprise inside one long chat. They said something important fifty messages ago. The later reply ignores it. Sometimes the product only sends a recent slice of the thread. The older lines are off the desk even though Sam can scroll up and see them. What Sam can scroll to, and what the model is shown, are not always the same pile. When you are unsure, paste the important lines again, close to the question. You are putting them back on the desk on purpose.

## What to write down tonight

Take a piece of paper, or a notes app, and write five lines Sam should have written before closing the laptop:

- The job: a one-page study brief.
- The three links, copied in full.
- The rule: each source gets one sentence.
- The rule: every claim names its source.
- The question the brief must answer, in Sam's words.

That paper is the drawer. Tomorrow, the first thing Sam pastes is that paper. The chat does not "continue." Sam rebuilds the desk. The reply can then be about the brief, because the brief's facts are visible.

Notice what this lesson is not asking you to build. It is not asking for a database, and it is not giving the drawer a special name. A drawer is enough. The skill is to point at a new chat and list the papers on it. If yesterday is not one of those papers, yesterday is not in the answer, no matter how friendly the greeting is.

Try it once if you want a felt version. In any free chat, say your name and a plan in one sentence. Close the thread. Open a new one. Ask it to repeat the plan. Then paste the sentence and ask again. The two replies are the picture, with and without the paper on the desk.

## Rebuild a desk in five minutes

Pick a plan you actually care about, small enough to fit on a card. Write the card before you close anything. The next time you open a chat, paste only the card and one question. Resist the urge to add "you remember, we talked." That sentence is a wish aimed at a broom. After the reply, underline every fact in it that came from the card, and circle every fact that was not on the card. The circles are continuations. Some circles may be harmless. Some may be the fake citation from the previous lesson, arriving as a detail you did not supply.

Do this twice, once with the card and once, on a throwaway question, without it. The pair of replies is the picture in words. The desk with the card can continue your plan. The desk without the card can only continue the single sentence you typed. If a product later claims to remember you across chats, test it with a fact that appears on no screen in the new chat. If it cannot say the fact, the claim is a greeting. If it can, something placed the fact on the desk for you. Your job is still to know which paper that was.
