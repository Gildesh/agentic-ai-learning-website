Sam sits down with one helper and two tools: open page, and save note. Three links are on the desk, pasted by Sam. The finish line from the earlier lesson is on the desk too: one page; each source named; one sentence per source; every claim has a source; if a link did not open, the page contains a written gap that names the link.

Sam is not building a Sunday machine yet. Sam is walking one pass slowly enough to see where a confident ending cheats.

## The plan is a list, and the list may change

Before any tool runs, the helper writes a short plan: open link 1, open link 2, open link 3, then draft the page, then save note. A plan is a list the helper may rewrite after it looks. It is not a promise that the world will cooperate. It is a sketch of the next actions, held lightly.

Call one pass a beat. A beat is: see the desk, choose, act, read what came back. Then the desk is different, and a new beat can begin. You can do this with a pen. Draw four boxes in a ring. You do not need a product.

## The walk

Beat one. The desk shows the plan and three links. The helper chooses open page. The input is link 1, an article about sleep and recall. The result comes back: several paragraphs of the article. Those paragraphs join the desk. The plan can stay as it is.

Beat two. The helper sees the new paper and the remaining links. It chooses open page for link 2, a short note on naps. The result comes back: the note's text. The desk is richer. Nothing has failed.

Beat three. The helper chooses open page for link 3, a library page. The result comes back as a failure: the page did not open. The failure is a paper on the desk, just as the articles were papers. It is news. The plan is allowed to change. The finish line is not. The finish line already said a missing link needs a written gap. The new plan says: draft one sentence for link 1, one sentence for link 2, and a gap line that names link 3. Do not invent a finding for the page that did not open.

That change is the point of an agent, in the sense you met earlier. The next step depended on what came back. A recipe would have kept drafting three findings because the card said three. The plan bent. The test did not.

## Then it says Done

The helper writes a draft. It has a sentence for link 1 and a sentence for link 2. It does not write the gap line. It says, "Done!" The draft is pleasant. The finish line is not met. The gap is missing, so a second person with the checklist would mark fail.

"Done!" is the model's opinion. It is the side door on the picture. The ring of a beat goes see the desk, choose, tool result returns, and back to see the desk. The side door is labeled "model says stop." It does not pass through the finish line. The helper stopped because the pattern of a finished answer includes the word done. The checklist was on the desk and was not applied.

Read the picture until you can say which door the helper used. The honest next beat would see the draft, notice the missing gap, and choose to add the line, then choose save note only after the line exists. The result of save note would be the word saved, or a failure. "Done" would still not be the evidence. The saved result plus a draft that includes the gap would be closer to the finish line. Even that pair is Sam applying the line, or a later checker applying it. The word done is never the line.

## What may change, and what may not

When link 3 fails, the plan may change. The finish line stays. The desk should gain the failure result, not a silent skip. If the failure is not on the desk, the next beat cannot "remember" that the library page broke. You already know a new pile does not contain what was never placed on it. Write the failure down where the next choice can see it.

Sam can walk this with index cards on a table. One card is the finish line. Three cards are links. When link 3 "fails," Sam flips that card to a red side that says "did not open." The plan card gets a new line. If the helper card says done while the red card has no gap sentence, Sam does not accept the draft. The theater is the lesson. The acceptance rule is the finish line, not the helper's mood.

You are still using one helper. That helper writes and also announces the ending. You can already feel the strain. The same voice that wants to be finished is the voice declaring the finish. A later lesson splits those jobs. Do not split them yet. Feel the strain, and keep the checklist in your own hand for this walk.

If you do this in a real chat that has no tools, you can still rehearse. Paste the three "results" yourself, including a failure for link 3, and ask for the draft. Then apply the checklist with your pen before you believe the last line of the reply. You are the finish line, sitting outside the model, which is exactly where this lesson leaves you.

## Walk the failure with cards

If you cannot picture the beats, use four index cards. Card A is the finish line, including the gap rule. Cards B and C are the texts that came back from link 1 and link 2. Card D starts as a link and is flipped to say "did not open" when link 3 fails. A fifth card is the plan. When D flips, you edit the plan card. You do not edit card A.

Now write a cheerful ending on a scrap: "Done, the brief is ready." Lay it on the table. Look at card A and look for the gap sentence. It is not in the draft. The scrap does not cancel card A. Throw the scrap aside. The next honest action is to write the gap, then to imagine a save-note result that says saved. Only then does the table match the finish line. If you skip the gap because the scrap was confident, you have used the side door. Practice throwing the scrap away until the gesture is boring. Boring means you will do it when a real reply is flattering.
