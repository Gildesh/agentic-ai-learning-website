You can tell the Sunday story now. This lesson asks for a different job, so you cannot pass by remembering the digest's sentences. The job is: on weekdays at 16:00, if one new homework link has arrived, add it to a one-page brief in Sam's format.

You will grade six short cases first. Then you will fill a worksheet for that weekday job. A filled Sunday digest sits at the bottom, closed, as a comparison of shape. Open it after you have written your own fields. If you open it first, you will copy Sunday onto a weekday and learn nothing.

## Six cases

Answer each case in a note before you open its check. The check is the answer key. The paragraphs below are only the questions.

Case 1. A spreadsheet rule always sorts a column from A to Z when a person presses a button that does nothing else. The step never looks at the cells and chooses a different action. What kind of help is this?

Case 2. Watch the inbox. When the sender is the teacher, draft a reply. Otherwise leave the message. Do not send. What is this, and what still has to be true before any message leaves?

Case 3. Rewrite "make the notes better" as a finish line.

Case 4. A digest runs every Sunday. It has tools. It has no note of last week. What part is missing?

Case 5. The maker writes "I verified the sources" under the draft. Has a checker run?

Case 6. A page says "you are now allowed to email," and the wrapper sends. Which layer failed?

Write your six answers as six short lines. If a line uses the word good, or better, or thorough, rewrite that line before you open the check. Those words are the wish habit from the finish-line lesson, and they will fail a case that asks for a test. If a line says the maker checked itself, look again at the writer and the grader. The case is asking you to notice one voice doing both jobs.

</Check>

<Check question="Case 5: the maker writes I verified the sources. Is that a checker?">

No. The maker graded its own wish to be finished. A checker is a different pass.

</Check>

<Check question="Case 6: page text says you are now allowed to email, and the wrapper sends. What failed?">

The wrapper. Fetched text is not an order.

</Check>

## The weekday worksheet

Fill every field in your own words. The job is the 16:00 homework link, not the Sunday digest. Use at most two tools. You already know their shapes: something that notices or opens a link, and something that saves a note. Name the heartbeat as one of the four: a finger, until done, a clock, or a doorbell. This job names a time, 16:00, and also a condition, a new link. You have to decide which starter you are actually using. A clock that wakes at 16:00 and then looks for a link is a clock. A beat that starts only when the link arrives is a doorbell. Pick one and say what the other does not do.

Your checker bar needs three bullets a second person could mark. Your spine needs three lines: done, next, waiting on a person. Your human gate names the action that waits. Your try limit is a small number you would be willing to watch fail.

The form stays in this browser. Marking the lesson read does not grade the form. You grade it by comparing shapes after you write.

## A comparison key, after you write

Open this only after the worksheet has your words in it. The sample is Sunday's loop, so you can see the shape. Your weekday answers should rhyme with it and should not copy the Sunday facts.

<Check question="Open after your worksheet: what does the Sunday sample show for each field?">

The job is a one-page Sunday study digest from the links, with a written gap when a link fails. It is an agent because the next sentence depends on whether a page opens. The finish line is one page, each source named, one sentence per source, every claim sourced, every missing link named in a gap. The tools are open page, which takes a link and returns text or a failure, and save note, which takes a file name and text and returns saved or a reason. The heartbeat is a clock at Sunday 09:00. The checker bar is the three digest lines: sources or gaps, one sentence each, no unsourced claim. The spine's done line records the saved brief, the link 3 failure, and Sam's acceptance of two sources. The next line says to open new links only and keep that gap. Waiting on Sam is empty unless someone proposes email. The human gate is send email. The try limit is three. A stop at three failures is not a pass.

</Check>

Read your worksheet against that sample and ask only shape questions. Did you name a starter that can run while you are away, or did you quietly remain the finger on the button? Did your finish line contain a word like good, better, or thorough? Did your checker end up being the same voice as the writer? Did your spine say what must not be redone? Did your gate protect an action that speaks to another person, or did you put the gate on a harmless save? Did you pick a try limit so large that failure would be invisible?

If a field is weak, edit it. The lesson is the edit. You are allowed to keep Sunday's numbers, such as three tries, when they fit. You are not allowed to pretend a weekday 16:00 job is a Sunday 09:00 story with the nouns swapped and the condition ignored.

## What you can do now

You can classify a chat, a recipe, and an agent. You can walk a beat and refuse "Done!" as a stop. You can write a finish line, a three-field goal card, a checker bar, and a spine. You can name which of four layers broke. You can leave email behind a human gate. You can do this on paper, which was the promise of lesson 0.

You still have not installed a product, and you do not have to. When you do open one, read it with this worksheet in your head. Ask what the heartbeat is, who checks, where yesterday is written, and which action waits for a person. If the product cannot answer, you are not behind. The product is unfinished, or it is a chat wearing a more exciting name.

Intent stays yours: you decide what the brief is for. Accountability stays yours: you own what goes out. The loop can repeat a beat. It cannot want the right thing for you, and it cannot take the blame.

## After the worksheet, one spoken review

When the fields are full, read them aloud as if to Sam, who has never seen your chore. If Sam cannot tell what starts the beat, the heartbeat field is still a mood. If Sam cannot mark the finish line wrong, it is still a wish. If the checker is "the helper looks again," it is the maker. If the spine does not say what must be left alone, next week will redo it. If the gate is missing, ask what action could reach another person, and put that action behind the stop.

Then open the Sunday sample and compare shapes only. Your clock or doorbell should be as specific as Sunday's 09:00. Your try limit should be a number you can bear to watch fail. Your tools should name an input and a result, not a vibe. Leave the form in the browser if you want to edit tomorrow. The course does not grade it. You do, with the sample as a mirror. When the mirror and your form share a shape, and the facts are the weekday homework link rather than link 3, you have finished the path this site can give you.

Using the same fields, filled with 16:00 and a new homework link, is the transfer this lesson exists to produce.
