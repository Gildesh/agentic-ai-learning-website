The draft has a sentence for link 1 and a sentence for link 2. Link 3 never opened. The finish line requires a written gap. The same helper that wrote the draft adds a line at the bottom: "I checked and it's good." There is no gap sentence. The helper has marked its own missing source as fine.

You have seen this side door. This lesson gives the two jobs different names so you can stop asking one voice to do both.

## Two passes

The maker is the pass that writes the draft. It is allowed to want the draft to be finished. That want is useful. It keeps the sentences moving.

The checker is a different pass that grades the draft against a written bar. It does not share the maker's wish to be finished. It returns pass or fail. It does not rewrite the draft in the same breath, and it does not get to call a missing line a matter of taste. If you want repairs, the failure goes back to the maker for another try, inside the try limit you already set.

Asking the maker to "double-check" keeps one judge. The same wish that skipped the gap line is the wish that reviews the skip. A second paragraph that says "I looked again" is still the maker. A checker is a different pass. Different means it is graded against the bar, not against the maker's hope, and it is not the same continuation congratulating itself.

## The three bars for Sam's digest

Write the bar as three lines the checker can mark.

One: three sources, or a written gap for each missing one.

Two: one sentence per source.

Three: no claim without a source.

Those are the bars. A checker that says "8 out of 10, feels solid" has left the bar. A score without a line to fail is a wish. You already rewrote wishes into tests. The checker is where the test sits down and works.

A model's score is weaker than a test a machine can run. A date format can be checked by a rule that does not involve a model at all: the characters are either "12 May" or they are not. The digest's sentences cannot be fully checked that way, because a sentence can name a source and still twist what the source said. So the checker here is a careful reader with a short bar, not a perfect instrument. Because it is weaker than a machine test, a human still sees the brief before it is sent. The human gate stays even after a pass, when the next action is email.

## What Sam says when the transcript lies

Read this line and refuse it: "I checked and it's good," written in the same chat that wrote the draft. Is that a checker? No. One pass graded its own wish to be finished. The repair is structural. A second pass receives the draft and the three bars, and nothing that says "please agree." Its only output is pass, or fail plus which bar failed.

If the bar that failed is "a written gap for link 3," the maker's next try has a narrow job. Add the gap. Do not polish the adjectives. Narrow failures are how a try limit stays honest. A vague failure, "make it better," burns the three tries on style.

Name one line that belongs on the digest bar, any time you are unsure the checker is real. If you cannot name a line, you do not have a checker. You have a fan of the draft.

The gate remains after the pass for a reason you can now say without mixing up the jobs. The checker answered: does this page meet the bar? Sam's wrapper answers a different question: may this page be emailed? A yes to the first is not a yes to the second. The picture puts the gate after the pass on purpose, so you do not treat a green mark as a stamp for the outside world.

## Fail one bar on purpose

Write a tiny draft that fails exactly one bar. Two sentences, sources named, and no gap for link 3. A real checker says fail, and it names the gap bar. It does not say the prose is lively. The maker's next try adds one line: "Link 3 did not open." The checker runs again and can pass, if the other bars hold.

Now write the fake version: the maker adds "I verified the sources" and does not add the gap. Nothing changed except the boast. If you feel a pull to accept the boast, that pull is the misconception. The bar is visible. The gap is absent. The boast is the maker. Say fail.

Keep the human gate in the scene even after the real pass. The page may now be saved. It may not be emailed. If your practice ends with "and then it sent itself," you skipped the picture. Put the gate back at the end of the sentence until the order is dull. Dull order is what you want on a Sunday when you are not watching every word.

If you want a single sentence to carry out of this lesson, use this one: the boast is not the bar. You can tape it above the place where you read drafts. Any line in the draft's own voice that says the draft passed is a boast until a different pass marks the three bars. The gap line is still either on the page or not. Your eyes can do that job today. A second pass should do it later, so Sunday does not depend on your eyes every time. Until that pass exists, your eyes are the checker, and they should be suspicious of praise.

Try the boast test on a paragraph you like. If you like it, you will want it to pass. That want is the maker's want, sitting in you. The bar does not care that you like it. Count the sentences. Look for the gap. If the gap is missing, the paragraph fails while remaining likable. Liking is allowed after the pass, as taste. It is not allowed to stand in for the pass. Write fail in the margin once, on a paragraph you like, so the feeling is familiar.

<Recap>

- The maker writes. The checker grades. They are different passes.
- The digest bar is three lines: sources or gaps, one sentence each, no unsourced claim.
- A score without a bar is a wish. A self-check is the maker again.
- A pass does not open the human gate. Email still waits for Sam.
