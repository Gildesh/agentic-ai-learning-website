Sam takes the finish line from the lesson about wishes and tests, and adds a heartbeat. The words are plain: repeat the beat until that line is true, or until three tries, whichever comes first. The line is still the boring checklist. One page. Each source named. One sentence per source. Every claim has a source. A written gap if a link did not open. The new part is the repetition, and the number three.

## The finish line is the heartbeat

You met "until done" as one of four starters. This is that starter, opened up. A clock rings because time passed. This heartbeat rings because the checklist is still false, and the try count is still under the limit. The work inside each try is a beat you can already walk: see the desk, choose, act, read the result. Then someone applies the line. If the line fails, another beat begins. If the line passes, the repetition stops. If the third try fails, the repetition also stops, and the stop is not a pass.

The model's "Done!" is not the line. You have seen that side door. A goal-shaped loop that treats "Done!" as success has put the writer in charge of the grade. The card you are learning to fill does not have a field called "stop when it sounds finished."

## A card with three fields

Write this card on paper. It is the whole product lesson.

Finish line: the checklist, in words a second person can mark.

Try limit: three.

Who checks: not the same pass that wrote the draft. A different pass, or Sam, with the checklist in hand.

Cursor's `/goal` is one product that takes a card like this. You hand it a finish line. It works. A check runs. It tries again or it stops. You do not need to install Cursor to understand the card. You do not need a tour of its switches. If you meet `/goal` later, ask whether the three fields are filled. A box that says "keep going" without a markable line is a wish with a motor.

Fill the card badly on purpose, once, so you can see the motor waste itself. Finish line: be thorough. Try limit: five. Who checks: the same helper, using its own sense of thorough. Why can this stop on a bad brief? Because "be thorough" cannot fail a check. The loop can treat a wish as success on try one. The extra four tries are never forced to repair a missing gap, because a missing gap does not make "thorough" false. The try limit never even becomes the story. The wish graduated immediately.

Now fill it well. Finish line: the checklist, including the gap line for link 3. Try limit: three. Who checks: a different pass that only returns pass or fail against the checklist. The check fails twice, because the gap line is missing, then the draft gains the line and the check passes. What happens? It stops on the third try because the finish line is true. That stop is a pass. If all three tries fail, it stops because the try limit hit, and the brief is not a pass. Sam should see a failed card, not a cheerful brief that pretends the limit was a kind of success.

## What the limit is for

A finish line can be impossible. The library page may never open, and the draft may keep "forgetting" the gap line because the desk is messy. Without a try limit, "until true" is a machine that does not end. Three is Sam's number for this digest. It is small on purpose. A small limit makes failure visible. A limit of fifty hides a stuck loop inside a long afternoon. You can raise a limit later, after you have seen real passes. You should not start with a limit you are unwilling to watch.

The person who writes the card still owns two things the card cannot invent. The first is intent: Sam wanted a study brief, not an email to the teacher. The second is accountability: if a bad brief goes out, the card does not stand in front of the teacher and take the blame. The wrapper's human gate remains, for the send. The card governs repetition. It does not govern permission. A pass on the third try is allowed to be saved. It is not allowed to become an email by itself.

When you read about `/goal`, picture this card on the table. Finish line. Try limit. Who checks. If a demo skips the third field, assume the writer is grading itself until you see a different pass.

## Fill the card twice

Draw the card twice on one sheet.

Card one, the bad card. Finish line: be thorough. Try limit: five. Who checks: the writer. Imagine a draft with no gap line. Ask whether card one can fail it. It cannot. The loop stops early and calls the wish a success. Write "not a pass" in the margin so the word success does not stick.

Card two, the working card. Finish line: one page, sources named, one sentence each, claims sourced, gaps written. Try limit: three. Who checks: a different pass. Walk a story where try one fails, try two fails, try three passes. The stop is a pass, on the third try. Walk a second story where all three fail. The stop is the limit. The brief is not a pass. Both stories use the same card. The difference is the checklist's result, not the model's mood.

If you meet a button called `/goal`, lay card two on top of the button in your mind. If the button cannot show the three fields, you do not know what it will repeat. Curiosity can wait. The card cannot.

<Misconception>

`/goal` means the agent decides when the work is good enough.

</Misconception>

The agent does not own "good enough." The finish line does. `/goal` is a product name for repeat until the finish line is true, or the try limit hits. If the line is a wish, the product will repeat a wish, or stop on a wish. The decision about quality was made when the line was written, not when the product looked confident.

Put the two cards where you will see them before you trust a repeating helper. The bad card is a warning label: a wish plus a motor. The working card is the one you are allowed to leave running, and only inside the try limit, and only with a different pass checking. If you can explain to a friend why card one graduates a bad brief and card two does not, you understand `/goal` well enough to recognize it in a product later. You still do not need the product's setup steps. The fields are the understanding.

<Recap>

- Until-done is a heartbeat: try again while the finish line is false and tries remain.
- The card has three fields: finish line, try limit, who checks.
- `/goal` is one product shape of that card, not a mood called good enough.
- A stop at the try limit is not a pass. A pass is the line coming out true.
